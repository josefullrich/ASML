Ranking across the chosen outputs

Ranked on

Per output and dataset, each ticked metric's badness (lower = better: |coverage − 0.9|, |log₂ width ratio|, 1 − τ …) is rescaled across all configs, then averaged over datasets → metrics → categories = that output's score. Overall = mean over the chosen outputs, only for configs that have every one. The scale is set by all configs, so filtering doesn't change a score. Lower is better.

Raw metrics, one output at a time

Reliability (PIT, pooled over datasets)

Share of truths below each predicted quantile. On the diagonal = calibrated; S-shape below/above on the left/right = too confident; the reverse = too cautious. Legend number = mean |gap|.

Coverage and width vs needed width

Top row: coverage of the central 10–99% intervals (diagonal = honest). Bottom row: width ratio = actual width ÷ the width this config needed (around its median) to contain that share of its own truths; 1 = right, > 1 too wide, < 1 too narrow (log scale). A config with big errors and big intervals sits at 1.

Metrics

crps
Continuous ranked probability score on the 101-quantile grid, in the output's units. Rewards being right and sharp. Lower.
coverage90
Share of truths inside the 5–95% interval. Target 0.90.
width90
Mean width of that interval, in the output's units. Lower, but only meaningful when coverage is right.
width_ratio90
Actual 90% width ÷ needed width (see above). Target 1.
width_error
Mean |log₂ width ratio| over the 10–99% intervals: 0 = right width at every level, 1 = off by 2× on average. Lower.
calib_error
Mean |observed − nominal| of the PIT over 5–95% (the reliability-plot gap). Lower.
coverage_curve_error
Mean |coverage − level| over the 10–99% central intervals. Lower.
width_adaptivity
Spearman correlation of interval width with |error|: does it widen where it's wrong? Higher.
kendall_tau
Kendall τ-b between predicted median and truth: does it order setpoints right (what BO uses)? Higher.
top10_recall
Share of the true top-10% rows also in the predicted top 10%. Meaningful for EUV (maximized), not for the constraint outputs. Higher.
rmse / mae / nrmse
Point errors of the mean / median, in units; nrmse = RMSE ÷ SD of the truth. Lower.
