What the fixes learned: Platt a, b and beta-binomial φ

Fitted at every step on the earlier predictions (mean over the chosen datasets). a > 1 sharpens the classifier's logit, < 1 softens it; b shifts it (b > 0 = raises p). φ is the beta-binomial concentration: large φ ≈ plain binomial, φ ≲ 2 = U-shaped (mass piles on 0/70 and 70/70). φ is on a log axis.

Metrics

Log score
−log P(the count that happened). The sharpest honest test: putting near-zero probability on what happened is punished hard. Lower is better.
Log score − entropy
The log score minus the binomial entropy at the observed count: the log score a model that knew the true yield would still average, because of counting noise alone. 0 = as good as physics allows. Same ranking as the log score (the floor is the same for every model on the same rows); it shows the room left.
CRPS
Σₖ (F(k) − 1[k ≥ ktrue])², in counts. Rewards putting probability near the truth, not just on it. Lower is better.
Coverage 90% (PIT)
Share of the truth's probability-integral transform inside the central 90%. For counts the PIT is spread uniformly over [F(k−1), F(k)] (computed exactly, no random draw), so a calibrated model scores exactly 0.90. Target 0.90.
Coverage 90% (interval)
Share of truths inside the central 90% count interval, endpoints included. Every model reads high on counts because the endpoint bins (the spikes at 0/70 and 70/70) carry extra mass, so it's shown for reference only.
Coverage error
Mean |PIT coverage − level| over 10, 20 … 99%: the whole confidence curve in one number. Lower is better.
Overdispersion
Σ deviance / Σ expected deviance, using the predicted mean yield. 1 = the misses are pure counting luck; much above 1 = the yield swings far more than a binomial with that mean allows. It judges the mean only, so the beta-binomial fix (which keeps the mean) leaves it unchanged — that is expected, not a bug; it is why the fix is needed.
